# Market research — question bank

Plain-English prompts per phase. Ask one or two at a time, always with a recommended answer the
user can react to. The framework terms in parentheses are for *you* — never push them into the
question. Adapt depth to `predicted_tier` and `technical_user`.

## Phase 1 — Define the market (demand-side)
- "Who exactly would buy or use this, and what job are they hiring it to do — in their words, not a category name?"
- If discovery ran: "Discovery already found the job is [X] — is that the job we're sizing?" (reuse it, don't re-derive)
- Reject: defining the market as "the [X] category" or by a demographic. Redefine by buyer + functional job; if the job is unclear, loop to `/discovery`.

## Phase 2 — Size it two ways (rank, don't forecast)
- Top-down: "Roughly how many buyers have this job, and what's the whole category worth — with a source?"
- Bottom-up: "Per customer, what's it worth (price × accounts, or users × revenue-each), and how many could you realistically win in a year?"
- "These two disagree — which do you defend, and why?" (the gap is the insight; the defensible number is bottom-up)
- For any figure the user can't give, offer to research it and mark it `TODO:`. Reject a size that's top-down only, and never let a "capture 1% of $X B" number stand.

## Phase 3 — Alternatives (status quo first)
- "If they don't buy anything, what do they do instead today?" (the do-nothing / current workaround — list it first)
- "Who else would they seriously evaluate — and is there a name that sounds like a rival but no buyer actually considers?" (cut the phantoms)

## Phase 4 — Competitive intelligence
- "For the top one or two, where do they genuinely win, and where do you?" — grounded in what buyers said, not their website.
- At prototype: "Desk research is fine here — I'll flag it as not buyer-validated. Agree?"
- Reject: loss reasons taken from the CRM field (≈85% are the seller's story, not the buyer's); a battlecard that changes no decision.

## Phase 5 — Macro / weak-signal scan (conditional)
- "Any big shift — regulation, technology, buying behaviour — that helps or hurts this over the next year or two?" (skip for a simple utility; human-validate any AI-surfaced trend and record its precision/horizon)

## Phase 6 — Positioning (Dunford order)
- "Given the alternatives: what can only you say (a real capability), what value does that unlock for the buyer, and who cares most?"
- "Do we position inside an existing category, or try to name a new one?" (category design is the rare exception — record the choice and why)
- Reject: a tagline or category we *want*, with no alternatives underneath it.

## Phase 7 — Floor + the call
- Re-check: are the competitive claims truthful, is the data sourced lawfully, is any AI-generated research labelled?
- "Given all this — is the market solid enough to feed the go/no-go (grounded), still thin somewhere (say where), or does it show a real viability problem (a signal for `/opportunity` or `/discovery`)?"
