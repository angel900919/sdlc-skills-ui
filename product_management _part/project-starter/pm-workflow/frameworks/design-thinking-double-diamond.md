# Design Thinking / Double Diamond

Human-centered problem-solving that **diverges to explore, then converges to decide — twice** (once on the problem, once on the solution). Design Thinking: **IDEO / Stanford d.school** (Empathize→Define→Ideate→Prototype→Test). Double Diamond: **UK Design Council** (Discover→Define→Develop→Deliver). (Canon: [`05_Conventions.md` §8](../05_Conventions.md); phase map: [`03_Frameworks_Map.md`](../03_Frameworks_Map.md).)

## What it's for / when to use
- Ill-defined ("wicked") problems where the *real* problem isn't yet clear.
- Early discovery and solution shaping — building empathy, generating and testing options before committing.
- When you need to deliberately separate *exploring the problem* from *exploring the solution*.

## The steps
Double Diamond (4 phases):
1. **Discover** — diverge in the problem space; research real user needs, don't assume.
2. **Define** — converge; reframe into a sharp problem statement / point of view.
3. **Develop** — diverge in the solution space; ideate many options, prototype.
4. **Deliver** — converge; test at small scale, kill what fails, refine what works.

(d.school's 5 modes — Empathize → Define → Ideate → Prototype → Test — are the same shape: iterative, not linear; loop back when new insight appears.)

## Worked micro-example
A bank assumes users want a budgeting feature. **Discover:** interviews reveal the real pain is anxiety about *surprise overdrafts*. **Define:** "Help paycheck-to-paycheck users avoid overdraft surprises." **Develop:** sketch 6 concepts (alerts, buffer, predicted balance). **Deliver:** paper-prototype the predicted-balance alert with 5 users → it tests best → refine.

## When NOT to use it
- Well-understood problem with a known solution — diverging just wastes time.
- No access to real users — the empathy step degrades into fiction.
- As a substitute for quantitative validation, prioritization, or viability — it's qualitative; pair it with JTBD-ODI, RICE, and a business case.

## Common mistakes / anti-patterns
- Treating it as a linear waterfall instead of an iterative loop.
- "Empathizing" from a workshop with no real research → **persona fiction** (decoration, not evidence).
- Ideating solutions before the problem is defined (converging the first diamond too early, or skipping it).
- **Prototype theatre** — prototypes nobody ever tests with real users.
- Stopping at "Deliver" once; in digital, solutions are never finished — keep looping.
