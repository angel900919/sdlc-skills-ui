# Continuous Discovery Habits

Make customer contact a **weekly habit, not a project** — small, frequent research by the team building the product, always tied to a desired outcome. **Frequency beats sample size.** Primary source: **Teresa Torres, *Continuous Discovery Habits* (2021)**. (Canon: [`05_Conventions.md` §8](../05_Conventions.md); phase map: [`03_Frameworks_Map.md`](../03_Frameworks_Map.md).)

## What it's for / when to use
- Keeping decisions grounded in *fresh* customer evidence at delivery speed.
- When discovery and delivery should run in parallel (dual-track), not as big upfront phases.
- The standing thread under the whole workflow — a living weekly layer.

## The steps
1. Set the **outcome** the trio is pursuing (tie discovery to one measurable outcome).
2. **Automate recruiting** so interviews happen weekly without a scramble.
3. The **product trio** (PM + design + eng) interviews ≥1 customer per week.
4. Interview for **stories** ("tell me about the last time…"), not opinions; capture an **interview snapshot** per interview.
5. **Synthesize continuously** onto an Opportunity Solution Tree; pick the next opportunity.
6. Run small **assumption tests**; feed the learning back. Repeat weekly.

## Worked micro-example
A scheduling app's trio commits to one interview every Tuesday. Over a month, snapshots surface a recurring story: people double-book because invites land in a *personal* calendar the work app can't see. That opportunity goes on the OST and becomes the next assumption test — no quarterly research project required.

## When NOT to use it
- One-off, high-stakes foundational ethnography or pricing / PMF studies — those need dedicated, rigorous research, not the weekly cadence.
- Teams with literally no market access, or under regulatory limits on user contact.
- Don't let "we interviewed N people" become the success metric — the metric is *decisions changed*.

## Common mistakes / anti-patterns
- Big upfront research, *then* build (the opposite of continuous).
- Counting interviews as the KPI; ad-hoc weekly recruiting scrambles.
- Direct / speculative questions ("would you use this?") and pitching in a discovery interview.
- Outsourcing discovery to a separate research team (breaks the trio's ownership).
- Manual transcript coding as the default — AI auto-tagging/summaries are table stakes in 2026; keep humans on bias, representativeness, and rapport.
