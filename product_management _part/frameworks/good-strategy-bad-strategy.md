# Good Strategy / Bad Strategy

A real strategy is a **kernel** of three linked parts — **Diagnosis → Guiding Policy → Coherent Action** — that confronts the single most important obstacle. Everything else (slogans, goals dressed as strategy) is *bad strategy*. Primary source: **Richard Rumelt**, *Good Strategy / Bad Strategy* (2011). Canon: [Conventions §8](../05_Conventions.md).

## What it's for / when to use
- Pressure-testing direction in **P01** (and the opportunity bet in **P04**) to confirm it's a real strategy, not a wish list.
- Cutting through a "strategy" that is actually a list of goals, financial targets, or motivational slogans.
- When the team can't answer "what is the one hard problem we're solving, and what are we therefore *not* doing?"

## The steps
1. **Diagnosis** — name the *crux*: the single most important challenge, and why it's hard. Simplify a messy situation down to its critical few facts.
2. **Guiding policy** — the overall approach that addresses the diagnosis (a method, not a goal). It rules things in *and* rules things out.
3. **Coherent actions** — a set of coordinated, mutually-reinforcing steps that carry out the policy. Resources concentrated, not sprinkled.
4. **Reject the hallmarks of bad strategy:** *fluff* (gluey jargon), *failure to face the challenge*, *mistaking goals for strategy*, *bad strategic objectives* (a dog's-dinner of unconnected aims).

## Worked micro-example
- **Diagnosis:** churn is high because new users never reach the "aha" — onboarding is a 9-step form. That, not pricing, is the crux.
- **Guiding policy:** compete on *fastest time-to-value*; remove every step between signup and first win.
- **Coherent actions:** kill the form (progressive profiling), ship a 60-second templated start, instrument time-to-first-value, and *defer* the new pricing page this quarter.

## When NOT to use it
- You need a *plan of layers* (mission → goals) — that's the [Product Strategy Stack](product-strategy-stack.md); Rumelt forges the core choice, the stack organises it.
- Pure execution/measurement work — use [OKRs](okrs.md) to track a strategy you've already diagnosed.

## Common mistakes / anti-patterns
- **Goals as strategy.** "Grow revenue 30%" is a target, not a strategy — it has no diagnosis or policy.
- **Fluff.** Abstract buzzwords ("synergistic AI-first platform") substituting for a choice.
- **No focus.** "Coherent actions" that are really the whole backlog — strategy is choosing what *not* to do.
- **Skipping the diagnosis** and jumping straight to solutions.
- **OKRs *as* the strategy** rather than the measurement of it.
