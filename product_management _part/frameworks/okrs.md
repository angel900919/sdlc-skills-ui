# OKRs (Objectives & Key Results)

A goal-setting system that pairs one qualitative, inspirational **Objective** ("where do we want to go?") with 2-5 measurable **Key Results** ("how do we know we're getting there?"), set on a cadence to align and focus teams on *outcomes*. Primary source: **Andy Grove** (Intel) → **John Doerr**, *Measure What Matters* (2018). Canon: [Conventions §8](../05_Conventions.md).

## What it's for / when to use
- Translating strategy into measurable outcomes in **P01**, then cascaded into delivery (**P10**) and tracked via analytics (**P12**).
- Aligning multiple teams on a shared, outcome-based direction each cycle (usually quarterly).
- When you need focus (a *few* goals) and a shared definition of "done well."

## The steps
1. **Write 1-3 Objectives** — qualitative, ambitious, time-bound, memorable. The "what/why."
2. **Define 2-5 Key Results per Objective** — measurable *outcomes* (a metric moving), not tasks/outputs. The "how we'll know."
3. **Align top-down + bottom-up** — leaders set direction; teams propose their own KRs (avoid a pure mechanical cascade).
4. **Check in on a cadence** — weekly/bi-weekly confidence updates; KRs are living, not filed-and-forgotten.
5. **Score & retro at cycle end** — ~0.7 is healthy for *stretch* KRs; learn, then reset. Decouple from compensation.

## Worked micro-example
- **Objective:** Make new users successful in week one.
- **KR1:** activation (reached first value) 38% → 55%.
- **KR2:** week-1 retention 22% → 30%.
- **KR3:** median time-to-first-value 3 days → 1 day.
- *(Note: "ship the onboarding redesign" is a task, not a KR — it belongs on the roadmap.)*

## When NOT to use it
- As a substitute for strategy — OKRs *measure* a strategy; they don't *make* the choice (use [Good Strategy / Bad Strategy](good-strategy-bad-strategy.md) + the [Product Strategy Stack](product-strategy-stack.md) first).
- For routine BAU / keep-the-lights-on work, or as a task tracker — that's a backlog, not an OKR.
- As an individual performance/comp instrument — it corrupts the goals.

## Common mistakes / anti-patterns
- **OKRs as strategy.** The #1 error: setting OKRs with no diagnosis or guiding policy above them.
- **Output KRs.** "Launch X / ship Y" instead of an outcome metric that moved.
- **Too many OKRs** — loss of focus; everything is a priority so nothing is.
- **Sandbagging** (easy KRs to guarantee 1.0) or **set-and-forget** (no check-ins).
- **Tying to bonuses**, which drives gaming and conservative targets.
