# Eliciting & Analyzing Requirements

> Domain: Requirements & Modeling · Learning order: 5 · Bloom target: Apply

## Overview

This topic covers the front end of requirements engineering: **gathering** stakeholder needs (elicitation), **classifying** them (functional, non-functional, constraint, domain), and **analyzing** them so they are clear, consistent, and feasible — then prioritizing and tracing each one back to its source (source: m2-elicit). "You can't engineer a solution until you understand the problem" is the governing idea (source: m2-intro). Elicitation and classification here vs *verifying* whether a requirement is well-written (SMART, testable) in topic 06; this topic produces and organizes requirements, [06-verifying-requirements](../06-verifying-requirements/README.md) judges their quality, and [07-requirements-management](../07-requirements-management/README.md) tracks them over time.

**What you already know that connects here:** [02-se-process-stages](../02-se-process-stages/README.md) places "define requirements" as an early stage of the SE process; this topic is the detailed how-to for that stage.

## Learning objectives

After this topic you will be able to:

- (Apply) Select an elicitation method (interview, survey, workshop, document review, observation) for a given stakeholder situation, weighing its advantages and challenges.
- (Apply) Rewrite a vague stakeholder statement into a clear, measurable requirement.
- (Apply) Classify a requirement as functional, non-functional, constraint, or domain.
- (Apply) Gather ≥5 requirements from distinct stakeholder types and attribute each to its source.
- (Apply) Assign High / Medium / Low / N/A priorities and resolve a conflict between two requirements.
- (Apply) Trace each requirement back to the stakeholder need and system goal it supports.
