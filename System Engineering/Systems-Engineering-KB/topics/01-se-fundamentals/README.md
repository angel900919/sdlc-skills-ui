# Systems Engineering & Core Principles

> Domain: SE Foundations · Learning order: 1 · Bloom target: Understand

## Overview

Systems engineering (SE) is an interdisciplinary approach to designing, integrating, and managing complex systems across their whole lifecycle, so that hardware, software, people, and processes work together to meet requirements and deliver value (source: m1-intro). This topic covers the governing ideas — what SE is, why it matters, systems thinking, the holistic view, its history, and the integrator role of the systems engineer. It defines *the mindset and the discipline* here; the concrete machinery built on that mindset lives in neighbouring topics — the five process stages in [02-se-process-stages](../02-se-process-stages/README.md), lifecycle models in [03-lifecycle-models](../03-lifecycle-models/README.md), and the SE tools catalog in [04-se-tools-techniques](../04-se-tools-techniques/README.md).

**What you already know that connects here:** nothing — this is the entry point with no prerequisites. If you have ever built or used a system whose parts had to cooperate (a phone, a car, a home network), you already have the intuition this topic formalises.

## Learning objectives

After this topic you will be able to:

- (Understand) Define systems engineering and explain its scope across the system lifecycle (source: m1-core).
- (Understand) Explain why SE matters by contrasting how a project unfolds with and without it (source: m1-intro).
- (Understand) Explain systems thinking and the holistic view, and classify a given concern as one or the other (source: m1-core).
- (Understand) Describe the historical milestones that shaped SE (WWI, Manhattan Project, Apollo) and the modern domains it now serves (source: m1-core).
- (Understand) Explain the integrator role of the systems engineer and why interdisciplinary integration is essential (source: m1-core).
