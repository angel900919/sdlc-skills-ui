# Requirements Management, Traceability & Change Management

> Domain: Requirements & Modeling · Learning order: 7 · Bloom target: Apply

## Overview

Once requirements are written and verified, they have to be kept alive across the whole system lifecycle: captured, tracked, traced to design/code/tests, and changed under control as the project evolves (source: m2-reqtools). Static documents like spreadsheets and Word files stop scaling as systems grow, so teams use **requirement management tools** (e.g. ReqView) plus **traceability** and **change management** processes to keep everything consistent (source: m2-reqtools). This topic is about *managing* requirements over time — distinct from topic 06, which is about *verifying* whether a single requirement is well written (SMART), and from topic 05, which is about *eliciting and analyzing* them in the first place.

**What you already know that connects here:** from [06-verifying-requirements](../06-verifying-requirements/README.md) you know how to judge a requirement as SMART and testable; here you keep those verified requirements traceable and under change control as they move into design, code, and test.

## Learning objectives

After this topic you will be able to:

- (Understand) Explain why requirements must be managed across the lifecycle rather than frozen in a static document.
- (Understand) Classify a traceability link as forward, backward (reverse), or bidirectional.
- (Apply) Build a small traceability matrix linking a requirement to its design, code, and test artifacts.
- (Apply) Walk a change request through the five-phase change management process.
- (Apply) Map the features of ReqView onto the requirements-management tasks they support.
- (Evaluate) Choose when bidirectional traceability is required versus when one direction suffices.
